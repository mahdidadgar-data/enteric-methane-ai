# 3-NOP: evidence from dairy-cow experiments

Source check: **4 September 2026**. This is a curated snapshot of two selected
PubMed-indexed studies, based on their abstracts. It is not a systematic review,
an exhaustive update, or a live search.

## What the selected studies report

- **Hristov et al. (2015):** A randomized experiment involving 48 lactating
  Holstein cows followed treatment for 12 weeks. Methane emissions and methane
  yield were approximately 30% below the control average. Feed intake and milk
  production were not reduced under these experimental conditions.
  [PubMed record, PMID 26229078](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26229078/).
- **Melgar et al. (2021):** In a separate 48-cow experiment lasting 15 treatment
  weeks, daily methane, methane per unit of intake, and methane per unit of milk
  declined by 26%, 27%, and 29%, respectively. Intake and milk yield were not
  affected; some milk composition measures changed.
  [PubMed record, PMID 33131815](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33131815/).

## Interpretation and limits

These experiments support methane mitigation in the studied dairy-cow settings.
They do not establish one universal response across diets, production systems,
or treatment durations. The reported percentages have different denominators;
they should not be combined into one effect estimate.

**Connection to this app:** Selecting the model's `3-NOP` category does not
reproduce either experiment. The model's normalized dose is not a physical
feeding dose. A negative model delta is not confirmation of a published effect.
The saved model has not been validated here against these studies.

Full-text assessment, study funding and risk-of-bias review, and qualified
nutrition advice are required before practical decisions. This brief is not a
feeding recommendation or a statement about current product authorization.
